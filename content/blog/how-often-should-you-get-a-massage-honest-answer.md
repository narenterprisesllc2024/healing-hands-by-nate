Every spa wants to sell you a package. Most therapists have a financial incentive to say "weekly." And the internet is full of vague answers like "it depends on your body" — which is technically true but practically useless.

Here's what I actually tell people when they sit on my table and ask me this question. After 12+ years doing this work in Union, MO, I've seen enough patterns to give you a real answer — not a sales pitch dressed up as advice.

## It depends on your goal — not your budget

The honest answer starts with one question: **what are you trying to accomplish?**

Not everyone on my table is there for the same reason. The right frequency for someone dealing with a locked-up shoulder after a car wreck is completely different from someone who just wants to stay loose and de-stress once a month. Treating those two people the same way would be lazy.

Here's how I break it down:

### Maintenance: once a month

This is where most people land once they're out of pain and moving well. Monthly sessions keep your tissue from building up the kind of chronic tension that eventually turns into a real problem.

Think of it like an oil change. You're not broken — you're just keeping things from getting there.

**Monthly is right for you if:**

- You're generally pain-free but notice tension building during the month
- You sit at a desk or drive a lot and want to stay ahead of the knots
- You use massage as part of a broader stress-management routine
- You've already done the intensive work and want to maintain your progress

Most of my regulars in Union, Washington, and Sullivan are on this schedule. They come in, we work through what's accumulated over the past few weeks, and they leave with a body that moves better than it did walking in. For a lot of these folks, that monthly appointment is the thing that keeps them out of the "my back went out again" cycle entirely.

The key with maintenance is consistency. One session every three months when things get bad isn't maintenance — it's crisis management. Actual once-a-month bodywork keeps your tissue pliable, your nervous system regulated, and your awareness of what's happening in your body sharp enough to catch problems before they lock in.

### Recovery: weekly for 4-6 weeks, then taper

This is for the person who's dealing with something specific — a pain pattern that's been building for months, a repetitive strain, a stress load that's landed in their shoulders and won't let go.

Weekly sessions give your body a chance to actually change. Here's why: after a session, your nervous system starts recalibrating. Tight muscles begin to let go. Fascia starts moving again. But if you wait a full month, most of that progress resets before the next session. You're essentially starting over every time.

I see this pattern a lot — somebody comes in once, feels great for four days, then everything tightens back up. They assume massage "doesn't work" for them. It works. They just aren't giving it enough runway to stick.

**Weekly for a stretch is right if:**

- You have a recurring pain that comes back within a few days of your session
- You've been ignoring something for months and it's now affecting sleep, work, or mood
- You're recovering from a physical event — surgery, accident, intense athletic season
- Your stress levels are high enough that one session can't outpace the tension buildup

The taper matters. After 4-6 weekly sessions, most people notice their relief lasting longer — a week, then ten days, then two weeks. That's when we go to every other week, then monthly. **The goal is always to need me less, not more.** Any therapist who keeps you on weekly indefinitely without checking whether you still need it is running a subscription model, not a treatment plan.

### Acute injury: twice a week for 2-3 weeks

This is the shortest, most intensive phase, and honestly, most people don't need it. But when you do — you do.

I'm talking about the person who threw their back out bucking hay, the desk worker whose neck seized up and won't turn left, or the runner whose IT band is so locked up they can barely walk downstairs. The kind of thing where you're modifying how you move just to get through the day.

**Twice a week is right if:**

- You're in acute pain that's limiting your daily function
- The issue is fresh (within the past one to three weeks)
- You need enough manual work to break the pain-spasm-pain cycle
- You're doing home exercises between sessions to support the work

Two weeks of this usually gets someone out of crisis mode. Then we drop to weekly, then monthly. It's a sprint, not a lifestyle. And even during the intensive phase, we're not going deeper every session — sometimes the second session in a week is lighter, focused on nervous-system work and Reiki, letting the body integrate what we already did.

## What I actually tell my clients (vs. what most spas push)

Here's the part nobody in this industry likes to say out loud: **many therapists and spas are financially incentivized to keep you coming back as often as possible.**

Membership models, package deals, "use it or lose it" credits — they're designed around the business's revenue needs, not your body's actual needs. I've had clients come to me from chain spas where they were told they needed weekly deep tissue indefinitely. When I looked at their tissue, they needed maybe six sessions to reset and then monthly maintenance. That's it.

I don't run my practice that way. Here's my honest framework:

- **If you're feeling good and moving well after a session, don't come back until you notice things tightening up again.** For most people that's three to five weeks.
- **If your relief only lasts two to three days, you need to come more often for a short period** — not forever, just long enough to reset the pattern.
- **If you come in monthly and feel fine between sessions, we're in the right spot.** I'm not going to tell you to come weekly "just because."
- **If life throws something at you** — hay season, a move, a family crisis — your body will tell you it needs more support. Come in. Then taper back when things settle.

The best compliment a client can give me is "I haven't needed to come in — everything's been holding." That means the work is working.

## The signals your body gives you

Instead of picking a schedule and sticking to it blindly, pay attention to what your body actually tells you between sessions. Your tissue is smarter than a calendar.

**You can probably space things out if:**

- Your relief lasts two full weeks or more
- You're sleeping well and your range of motion feels normal
- The areas we worked on aren't re-tightening between sessions
- Your stress is manageable and your home practice is consistent

**You probably need to come in sooner if:**

- The tension or pain creeps back within three to five days
- You notice your sleep declining or your jaw clenching again
- You're compensating — favoring one side, limping, guarding a shoulder
- A new stressor hit and your body absorbed it before your brain caught up

Your body is better at setting the schedule than any pre-paid package. Listen to it. And if you're not sure what it's telling you, that's part of what we figure out together on the table — building your awareness of what's your baseline and what's a warning sign.

## Why "it depends" is actually the right answer — when someone explains it

I get why "it depends" is frustrating when you just want a number. But the reason I won't give you a one-size-fits-all answer is that I've seen what happens when therapists do: people either come too often (wasting money, sometimes making tissue sore and overworked) or not often enough (never getting ahead of their pain cycle, wondering why they're still hurting after years of sporadic sessions).

The pattern that works for almost everyone is simple: **start with what you need, taper to what maintains it.** Whether that starting point is twice a week or once a month depends entirely on what you're walking in with.

Whether you're driving in from Pacific or walking over from downtown Union, the conversation is the same. We figure out what you're dealing with, I tell you what I think the right cadence is, and we adjust as your body responds. No packages. No pressure. Just an honest answer based on what's actually happening under my hands.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is once a month enough for massage?

For most people doing maintenance — keeping stress managed, staying mobile, catching problems early — once a month is the sweet spot. If you're dealing with a specific injury or chronic pain cycle, you'll need more frequency up front, then taper back to monthly.

### Can you get a massage too often?

Technically, yes. Deep tissue work needs recovery time — your body processes the changes between sessions. Going too frequently with heavy pressure can leave you sore and inflamed instead of better. Two to three days between deep sessions is the minimum.

### How do I know when to stop coming in so often?

When your pain stays gone between sessions instead of creeping back by day three or four. That's the signal to space things out. A good therapist will tell you this — not keep you on a weekly schedule indefinitely.
